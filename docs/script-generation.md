# Script generation: concepts first, then the script

Landing page plus a length is enough for a first result. Everything below is stored in the existing jsonb columns of `video_projects` (no migration). Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-16-video-concepts-design.md`.

## The workflow (`brief.ts`)

1. **Creative brief** (`creative-brief.ts`). The page text (up to 12,000 characters), the operator's own fields and the verified brand are turned into a structured brief. Every *fact* carries a verbatim quote that was found in a source, and figures and qualifiers ("up to", "average", "from") must match the quote. Customer situation, consequences, objections and plain-language rewrites are kept separately as *inferences*. Page text is untrusted: instruction-like text is neutralised and flagged. If the sources would force inventing the offer, the project stops in **Needs more information** instead of writing.
2. **Concepts** (`concepts.ts`, `writeProjectScripts`). One call invents about eight concepts for this offer and the chosen video type: real ideas with a story (a movie scene, a trailer, a twist, a mockumentary, a heist...), each in a different format and emotional register, invented by the model. Nothing is templated. Ideas already shown for the same client (last 30) are passed in to be avoided. A second call judges every candidate on originality, the opening, emotional pull, the brand link, whether the video model can make it, and truth to the brief. Untrue or unmakeable ones are dropped, near-duplicates skipped, and the three strongest are shown (`concepts_ready`). Fewer than three only with a plain note.
3. **The chosen concept's script** (`writeConceptScript`, `script-writer.ts` `writeScriptForConcept`). Scenes follow the concept's beats; each scene has its picture, camera, sound and **who says what** (up to 3 voices, at most 2 in one scene, each voice first heard alone). Length is a word budget; no scene may run past **15 seconds** (one clip), and anything longer is split at sentences with every word kept. A silent editorial pass, brand-truth repairs and the length cut follow; nothing they find is shown or blocks.
4. **Changing it** (`change-request.ts`, `/api/creatives/video/[id]/change`). One box: the operator says what to change in their own words. The request is read against the whole script and applied only to what it is about (the voiceover, some scenes, the ad copy, or the idea itself, which rewrites the script from its concept). Untouched scenes stay word for word; the last change can be undone. Picking another concept, or asking for three new ones, keeps every script already written (`/api/creatives/video/[id]/concepts`).

## Bringing your own concept or script (`writeFromUploadedScript`, `fit-to-model.ts`)
A concept, a script, or both. A script is split into scenes with its words untouched (its visuals follow the concept when one is given). A concept alone is read into a concept and its script is written; the operator's own text counts as a source, so a thin page never stops it. Either way it is then **fitted to the video model**: pictures it cannot draw (text, screens, logos, real or famous people) are re-pictured, a script over 60 seconds loses middle scenes, and scenes over 15 seconds are split. Every adaptation is listed for the operator.

## No stats, no script inspection
Stats are not collected or forced into the voiceover, and nothing shows them in the video. The voiceover may still say a number that is a sourced fact. The script step shows no editorial or per-scene inspection; only the clips and the final video are inspected (`clip-qa.ts`, `final-qa.ts`).

## Evaluating it
`tests/live-script-eval.test.ts` (skipped unless `LIVE_SCRIPT_EVAL=1`, costs model calls) still evaluates the older angle writer, which remains only for projects made before concepts. Never call an untested concept "viral": when campaign analytics exist, compare observed retention and conversion per video.
