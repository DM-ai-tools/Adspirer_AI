# Script generation: how three different, human-sounding versions are made

Landing page plus a length is enough for a first result. Everything below is stored in the existing jsonb columns of `video_projects` (no migration).

## The workflow (`brief.ts`, `writeProjectScripts`)

1. **Creative brief** (`creative-brief.ts`). The page text (up to 12,000 characters), the operator's own fields, uploaded images and the verified brand are turned into a structured brief. Every *fact* carries a verbatim quote that was found in a source, and figures and qualifiers ("up to", "average", "from") must match the quote. Customer situation, consequences, objections and plain-language rewrites are kept separately as *inferences*. Results and testimonials carry their scope so one customer's outcome is never stretched into a universal promise. Page text is untrusted: instruction-like text is neutralised and flagged, and the model is told everything between the source delimiters is data. Missing context is listed (with one question each); material defaults (audience, platform, tone, goal, speaking pace) are shown and editable. If the sources would force inventing the offer, the project stops in **Needs more information** instead of writing.
2. **Angles** (`angles.ts`). The model proposes more candidate angles than needed, built from the actual offer: the customer situation, the tension, the central message, what the opening is about, the proof that carries it (real fact ids), the tone and the reason to act. A deterministic distance check (content words, so changing "struggling" to "tired" is not a new angle) selects three that differ in premise, opening and development. If the page cannot support three genuinely different angles, fewer are returned with a plain explanation. There is no fixed angle bank and no named copywriting formula.
3. **Writing** (`script-writer.ts`). Each angle is written on its own: several opening lines are considered, the strongest grounded one is chosen and must be paid off; the offer enters where it helps; the mechanism is explained from the facts; one real call to action matches the page's next step; sources are recorded per claim. The length is a budget (`duration.ts`): words from the requested seconds, the speaking pace and what must be reserved for pauses and the closing line. A draft that is long or short is edited (twice at most) — never fixed by making the narrator faster.
4. **Editorial pass** (`editorial.ts`, `script-quality.ts`). Deterministic detectors (default openings, stacked slogans, rhetorical-question habits, "not X but Y", lists of three, jargon, filler, fabricated experience, invented urgency / free offers / guarantees, CTA mismatch, repeated visual clichés, duplicate angles) plus a model review against the brief. Weak lines are rewritten (bounded passes); scenes the operator edited are never rewritten. Hard failures — unsupported claims, wrong brand information, a materially misleading length, fabricated testimonials, essentially duplicate angles — are never hidden. Model ratings are editorial signals, not predictions of performance.

## What each version shows
Angle name, the customer situation, one sentence on what makes it different, the complete spoken script (copyable), words and **estimated** spoken length (the **measured** length appears once a video exists, taken from the audio), visual direction, on-screen words, the closing line and its button, and where each claim comes from.

## Changing one version (`script-actions.ts`, `/api/creatives/video/[id]/versions`)
Write this version again · same angle, new delivery · different angle from the same facts · new length. The other versions are never rewritten, scenes the operator edited survive, and the new version goes through the same editor (compared against the untouched versions for overlap). Editing a script recalculates its length and speech estimate and re-checks changed figures. "Confirm this script" sends it to video generation as before.

## Evaluating it
`tests/live-script-eval.test.ts` (skipped unless `LIVE_SCRIPT_EVAL=1`, costs model calls) runs the old and the new generators on the same pages — specific, weak-copy, multi-brand, no-proof and unsupported-claims pages, at different lengths — and writes `.data/script-eval/`: `results.json`, `report.md` (automated checks side by side), and `blind-review.md` with old and new versions shuffled and unlabelled, plus `blind-key.json`. Automated checks are a safety net; the blind human read is the test. Never call an untested script "viral": when campaign analytics exist, compare observed retention and conversion for each version in its campaign context.
